§ 0

Mood and tense — two different axes

These two words are often conflated. They answer different questions.

Tense answers
When?
Past, present, future. Tense locates an action in time.
Mood answers
How real is it?
Is this a fact, a hypothesis, a wish, a command? Mood expresses the speaker's relationship to reality.

French has four moods. The indicatif states facts — most of the tenses you use daily live here. The subjonctif marks doubt, emotion, or necessity. The conditionnel marks hypotheticals — things that would happen under some condition. The impératif issues commands.

When you hear a verb ending, your ear is doing two jobs simultaneously: tracking when (tense) and tracking how real (mood). The endings encode both at once.
§ 1

Why the futur and conditionnel sound so similar

They share the same stem: the infinitive, which carries its ʁ into the conjugated form. The only difference is the ending. The futur attaches new endings; the conditionnel attaches the imparfait endings to that same stem.

Futur simple
paʁlɛʁ.e

"I will speak." A real projection into future time. Indicative mood — you're asserting it will happen.

vs
Conditionnel
paʁlɛʁ.ɛ

"I would speak." A projection into a contingent or unreal situation. Sometimes called the futur du passé — the future as seen from within a condition that may never be met.

Same stem, different vowel. Both tenses project forward; the stem's ʁ carries that shared meaning. The ending resolves which: closed e → asserted future; open ɛ → contingent/hypothetical.
§ 2

The sounds that matter — minimal IPA key

Only a handful of sounds appear in verb endings. Here they are, with the phoneme-type coloring applied.

symbol type sounds like appears in (spelling — ref only)
e oral vowel "ay" in "say" — tense, closed, no diphthong ‑er ‑ez ‑é ‑ai (futur je)
ɛ oral vowel "e" in "bet" — open, mouth wider than [e] ‑ait ‑ais ‑aient ‑è ‑ê
a oral vowel open front "ah" ‑a ‑as (futur/passé simple)
ɔ̃ nasal vowel nasalized "o" — air through nose, no English equivalent ‑ons ‑ont
ʁ rhotic (French r) voiced uvular fricative — back of throat, never rolled r, rr — the futur/cond stem marker
j glide "y" in "yes" — brief, transitions into the following vowel ‑ions ‑iez (imparfait/subj. nous/vous)
The e / ɛ distinction is the most important one to train. e is tense and closed — futur, ‑ez. ɛ is open — imparfait and conditionnel. Many spellings map to the same sound: ‑ait, ‑ais, ‑aient are all ɛ.
§ 3

Présent — the bare stem

No tense marker at all. You hear the stem, then a person sound. For -er verbs the singular ending is silent — paʁl — so je, tu, il, and ils all sound the same.

Présent · parler
pronounwhat you hear (IPA)spelling (ref)
je / tu / il / ils paʁl je parle · tu parles · il parle · ils parlent
nous paʁlɔ̃ nous parlons
vous paʁle vous parlez
The ‑ent of ils/elles is completely silent for -er verbs. Four pronouns (je / tu / il / ils) all produce the same sound paʁl. The plural is signaled only by context or the pronoun.
§ 4

Imparfait — inject ɛ

Past ongoing or habitual action ("I was speaking," "I used to speak"). Built from the nous present stem (drop ɔ̃) by inserting the open vowel ɛ. No exceptions, ever, across all French verbs.

Imparfait · parler
pronounwhat you hear (IPA)spelling (ref)
je / tu / il / ils paʁlɛ je parlais · il parlait · ils parlaient
nous paʁljɔ̃ nous parlions
vous paʁlje vous parliez
The spelling trap: parlais, parlait, parlaient are three different spellings of one sound: paʁlɛ. Your ear only has one thing to learn here.
§ 5

Futur simple — hear the ʁ

Built on the infinitive, which keeps its ʁ audible. That ʁ inside a conjugated form is your signal: you're in futur or conditionnel territory.

Futur simple · parler
pronounwhat you hear (IPA)spelling (ref)
je paʁlɛʁe je parlerai
tu / il paʁlɛʁa tu parleras · il parlera
nous / ils paʁlɛʁɔ̃ nous parlerons · ils parleront
vous paʁlɛʁe vous parlerez
Person endings by sound: je/vous → e · tu/il → a · nous/ils → ɔ̃.
§ 6

Conditionnel — ʁ stem + ɛ endings

Same infinitive stem as the futur, but with the imparfait's ɛ endings attached. Once you know §4 and §5, this is automatic.

Conditionnel · parler
pronounwhat you hear (IPA)spelling (ref)
je / tu / il / ils paʁlɛʁɛ je parlerais · il parlerait · ils parleraient
nous paʁlɛʁjɔ̃ nous parlerions
vous paʁlɛʁje vous parleriez
Futur vs. conditionnel by ear: both have ʁ in the stem. Then — ending closed e / open ɛ for je; for nous/vous the glide j marks conditional, its absence marks futur.
§ 7

Subjonctif — mood, not tense

The subjonctif marks doubt, wish, emotion, or necessity — not a fact. Almost always preceded by que. Its endings are phonetically identical to the présent for -er verbs in the singular/ils, and identical to the imparfait for nous/vous. Context (the word que plus a trigger verb like vouloir, falloir, douter) is the primary cue.

Subjonctif présent · parler
pronounwhat you hear (IPA)spelling (ref)sounds like
je / tu / il / ils paʁl que je parle · qu'ils parlent = présent
nous paʁljɔ̃ que nous parlions = imparfait nous
vous paʁlje que vous parliez = imparfait vous
The subjonctif is phonetically distinctive only in irregular verbs, where the stem itself changes: aj for aller (que j'aille), swa for être (que je sois), ɛ for avoir (que j'aie).
§ 8

Passé simple — literary; recognize, don't produce

Completed past with a definite endpoint, used only in formal writing and literature. You'll read it constantly in novels; you'll never need to say it. Train your ear only to recognize it.

Passé simple · parler (‑er verb)
pronounwhat you hear (IPA)spelling (ref)
je paʁle je parlai
tu / il paʁla tu parlas · il parla
nous paʁlam nous parlâmes
vous paʁlat vous parlâtes
ils paʁlɛʁ ils parlèrent
Recognition cues: am / at for nous/vous are unmistakable in any verb. ɛʁ for ils is equally distinctive.
§ 9

Stem-changing irregulars — présent

These are the high-frequency verbs whose stem — not just the ending — changes in the présent. The endings follow the same rules; what shifts is the root sound itself. The pattern is called the "boot" pattern (or le radical variable): the changed stem appears in the singular + ils forms, and the unchanged stem appears in nous/vous.

verb je (IPA) nous (IPA) ils (IPA) spelling je / nous / ils what changes
être sɥi sɔ̃m sɔ̃ suis / sommes / sont stem changes completely — three distinct stems
avoir ɛ avɔ̃ ɔ̃ ai / avons / ont stem suppressed entirely in je/ils
aller vɛ alɔ̃ vɔ̃ vais / allons / vont two completely different stems (vais/va, all‑, von‑)
faire fɛ fzɔ̃ fɔ̃ fais / faisons / font stem vowel ɛ (sing) → ə (nous)
pouvoir pø puvɔ̃ pøv peux / pouvons / peuvent ø in sing/ils; u in nous/vous
vouloir vø vulɔ̃ vøl veux / voulons / veulent ø in sing/ils; u in nous/vous — same boot pattern as pouvoir
savoir sɛ savɔ̃ sav sais / savons / savent sing ɛ → nous/vous/ils a
venir vjɛ̃ vənɔ̃ vjɛ̃n viens / venons / viennent nasal jɛ̃ in sing/ils; schwa stem in nous/vous
tenir tjɛ̃ tənɔ̃ tjɛ̃n tiens / tenons / tiennent identical boot pattern to venir
prendre pʁɑ̃ pʁənɔ̃ pʁɑ̃n prends / prenons / prennent nasal ɑ̃ in sing/ils; ə+n in nous/vous
boire bwa buvɔ̃ bwav bois / buvons / boivent wa in sing/ils; u in nous/vous
croire kʁwa kʁajɔ̃ kʁwaj crois / croyons / croient wa in sing/ils; a in nous/vous
devoir dwa dəvɔ̃ dwav dois / devons / doivent wa in sing/ils; ə in nous/vous

The boot pattern by ear: for most of these verbs, the singular + ils forms share a stronger or more "open" stem vowel (ɛ ø wa nv{ɛ̃}), while nous/vous have a reduced or more closed stem (ə u a). The endings themselves are always regular. Train the stem pairs, not the full paradigm.
§ 10

Sound → meaning — the full decision map

You hear in the ending… Stem has ʁ? Tense / mood Meaning
bare stem (nothing) — Présent (je/tu/il/ils) fact, now or habitual
stem + ɔ̃ no Présent nous fact, now, we
stem + e no Présent vous fact, now, you (pl.)
stem + ɛ no Imparfait (je/tu/il/ils) past, ongoing
stem + jɔ̃ no Imparfait nous / Subj. nous past ongoing — or subjonctif (needs que)
stem·ʁ + e yes Futur (je / vous) will happen
stem·ʁ + a yes Futur (tu / il) will happen
stem·ʁ + ɔ̃ yes Futur (nous / ils) will happen
stem·ʁ + ɛ yes Conditionnel (je/tu/il/ils) would happen
stem·ʁ + jɔ̃ yes Conditionnel nous would happen, we
am or at or ɛʁ — Passé simple completed past, literary
The single decision tree: Hear ʁ in the stem? → futur or conditionnel. Ending closed e/a/ɔ̃ → futur. Ending open ɛ or with glide j → conditionnel. No ʁ? → présent or imparfait. Hear ɛ? → imparfait. Bare stem? → présent.
